ONE-PIEZO EXPERIMENT: COMPLETE CONNECTION TABLE

This is a preliminary test, not a verified final jammer. Before assembly,
confirm the exact piezo electrode assignments, piezo voltage rating, resistor
power rating, and 2N7000 manufacturer pinout. Photo-based color identification
is tentative: blue appears to be brass, green main electrode, yellow feedback.

Other three piezos                    Not connected

The 100 ohm resistor and main piezo are in parallel between +3.3 V and drain.
100 ohm resistor must be rated >= 0.25 W; maximum steady on-state dissipation
is 3.3^2 / 100 = 0.109 W. ESP32 GPIO must never receive 5 V. Use a separate
regulated 3.3 V supply with initial current limit 50 mA, and common ground.
If you have a second 100 ohm resistor, put it between GPIO18 and gate; the
10 kohm resistor remains connected between gate and ground.

Arduino ESP32 core 3.x: load esp32_test/esp32_test.ino. GPIO18 outputs
25 kHz at 50% duty. Check gate and drain voltages with an oscilloscope.
Piezo resonance may cause drain voltage to exceed the supply; a supply current
limit does not prevent all transient peaks. Stop if voltage exceeds component
ratings, or parts heat. The 2N7000 threshold does not guarantee its switching
performance at 3.3 V gate drive. Unknown piezo rating and impedance mean a
safe final voltage, output level and microphone interference are not assured.
Do not expose ears or animals to close-range ultrasound. Identify components
and measure waveforms before increasing voltage or connecting more piezos.
