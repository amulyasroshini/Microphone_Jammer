3.3 V ONE-PIEZO TEST (PROVISIONAL)

USB powers the ESP32 development board. If the board documentation confirms
its 3V3 pin supplies external loads and its regulator has enough spare
capacity, connect 3V3 to the piezo and 100 ohm resistor supply node. The 3V3
pin is a hardware power rail, not a GPIO, and requires no code to turn it on.
Never use a GPIO as the piezo supply. Do not connect an external 3.3 V supply
in parallel with that pin. Alternatively use a separate regulated 3.3 V supply
for the piezo; connect only its negative to ESP32 GND.

GPIO18 -> MOSFET gate; gate -> 10 kohm -> ESP32 GND; source -> ESP32 GND.
3V3 -> 100 ohm >=0.25 W -> drain. Piezo main electrode -> 3V3; piezo brass
-> drain; leave feedback electrode insulated and disconnected. Do not connect
the other discs or unidentified diode. Green/main, blue/brass, yellow/feedback
are tentative photo-based identifications. Confirm using the datasheet, as well
as the piezo voltage rating and 2N7000 manufacturer pinout before connecting.

100 ohm can draw up to 33 mA continuously while the MOSFET is on; power can
reach 0.109 W. Transient piezo currents and ESP32 consumption are additional.
A weak regulator may cause resets or overheating. 3.3 V gate drive may not
fully enhance a particular 2N7000. Electrical waveform and interference are
unverified; piezo voltage can ring above the supply. Do not increase voltage
or add more piezos without suitable component ratings and measurements.
Code esp32_test/esp32_test.ino generates 25 kHz PWM on GPIO18; a constant
supply is provided by the board's 3V3 pin, not by a software-controlled GPIO.
