5 V ONE-PIEZO EXPERIMENT — PROVISIONAL, NOT A VERIFIED JAMMER

Do not connect the piezo until its main/feedback electrode identification and
voltage rating have been verified. Color identification from the photo is
only tentative: green main electrode, blue brass, yellow feedback. Verify the
2N7000 pinout from your exact part's manufacturer datasheet.

ESP32 development board: normal 5 V USB power. GPIO18 generates 25 kHz,
0-3.3 V, 50% duty, using esp32_test/esp32_test.ino (Arduino ESP32 core 3.x).
Separate regulated 5 V supply for piezo stage. Connect supply negative to
ESP32 GND. Do not connect separate supply positive to the ESP32 USB/5 V pin
while USB-powered; do not connect 5 V to GPIO or 3V3 pin.

CONNECTIONS
ESP32 GPIO18          -> 2N7000 gate
10 kOhm resistor      -> between gate and common ground
2N7000 source         -> common ground
ESP32 GND             -> common ground
5 V supply negative   -> common ground
5 V supply positive   -> 100 ohm resistor first end and piezo main electrode
100 ohm other end     -> 2N7000 drain and piezo brass electrode
Piezo feedback        -> leave disconnected and insulate
Unknown diode         -> leave disconnected
Three remaining discs -> leave disconnected

The resistor is parallel to the piezo. It must be at least 0.5 W: 5 V across
100 ohm gives 50 mA and 0.25 W continuously, so a 0.25 W part has no margin.
A 3.3 V gate may not switch a particular 2N7000 well. 25 kHz is not itself
too fast, but actual circuit waveform and current are unknown. A piezo may
ring above 5 V and supply current limiting cannot prevent every transient.
Do not raise the voltage or connect additional discs without ratings and a
waveform measurement. Do not claim ultrasonic generation or interference
without measuring. Avoid close exposure of people and animals to ultrasound.
The resistor circuit wastes power and this setup may yield weak/no interference.
